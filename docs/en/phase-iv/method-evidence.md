## Prospective calculation specified for review

Let `r[j,t]` and `r[ref,t]` be aligned net simple USD account returns, including cash periods and applicable execution, financing and recurrent costs under frozen accounting rules. An inference observation is one common calendar interval, not a coin, fill or overlapping trade. Let `d[t] = r[j,t] - r[ref,t]`, and fix the economic threshold `delta` in those same per-interval units before inspecting the evaluation returns. The claim is `H0: E[d] <= delta` against `H1: E[d] > delta`. Any absolute-return-versus-cash requirement remains a separate necessary economic gate; an incremental advantage can still lose money.

The [protocol](protocol.md) chooses a **paired stationary block bootstrap of the mean differential**. Random block boundaries avoid a single fixed segmentation; this is a prospective design choice, not evidence of universal superiority. Circular fixed-length blocks remain a documented alternative, not a second method to select by its p-value:

1. Freeze the common evaluation grid, admissible contiguous segments, mean block length rule, method/version, random generator, seed and `B = 9,999` replications. This replication count is a computational precision allocation, not a market sample-size requirement or profitability threshold. It permits an empirical p-value grid of 0.0001; it does not make inference exact.
2. For each comparison, retain observed `d_bar`, and construct centered columns `z[t] = d[t] - d_bar`. Resample the synchronized vector of all candidate/reference differentials using **the same time indices**. Never sample individual assets or strategies independently, since that destroys common shocks and dependence across comparisons.
3. For each admitted contiguous segment, draw its initial time index uniformly. At each following step, with probability `1/ell` restart at a uniformly drawn index of that segment; otherwise advance to the next index with end-to-start wrap-around. This gives geometrically distributed discrete block lengths with frozen mean `ell`; preserve the required sample count and fixed segment aggregation weights. The documentation describes the random-length family as exponential; the discrete restart rule here states the intended algorithm explicitly. Do not manufacture adjacency across exchange outages, omitted intervals or disjoint validation folds. A segment with unresolved nonstationarity is not made valid merely by wrapping it.
4. Compute `d_bar_star[b] = mean(z[indices_b])`; compare it with `d_bar - delta`. The one-sided Monte Carlo estimate is `(1 + number of d_bar_star[b] >= d_bar - delta) / (B + 1)`. The null boundary is imposed by centering. Resampling uncentered differentials and treating the exceedance as a null p-value would answer the wrong question. The `+1` convention prevents a zero estimate; unlike exact randomization tests, it does **not** certify exact finite-sample type-I error for an approximate block bootstrap.
5. Report the estimated effect and uncertainty in the original units, alongside the p-value and the frozen multiplicity adjustment. Report Monte Carlo resolution/uncertainty. An inference result sensitive to simulation noise at the decision boundary is inconclusive under the frozen decision policy; do not rerun seeds or increase replications until rejection occurs. The paired error distribution can also support a prespecified basic interval, but pointwise intervals must not be labelled simultaneous confidence bands.

This is a project protocol choice, not an assertion that an upstream page supplies a ready-made cryptocurrency test. It is an approximate conditional analysis of the frozen procedure's observed account returns. It does not rerun order-book execution, account for unobserved search, or estimate the distribution of every alternative training/selection history. The later implementation must be independently checked with deterministic synthetic cases and dependent null simulations before inferential claims are admitted; those simulations have not been executed in IV.

## Block length and applicability gate

IV-M02's selector is an option for a predeclared implementation. Fit its tuning on development data only; if final sample length enters a prespecified plug-in formula, that length may come from sealed metadata, not a final-return search. For joint comparisons, freeze a common length rule that covers the relevant dependence and overlapping holding/label horizons; a conservative maximum of admitted per-column estimates is one option, not a theorem of optimality. Record all estimation failures and zero/undefined long-run variance. Bind rounding, segment handling and any length cap explicitly before opening the final sample. A cap cannot silently force a barely identifiable problem into a conventional test.

Sensitivity at a shorter and longer block length may be specified prospectively, for example rounded half/twice the base length where feasible. This is a diagnostic axis, not permission to select the most favorable p-value. Keep the primary method/length fixed. If conclusions depend materially on admissible length choices, report fragility/inconclusive evidence and do not claim robustness. The final binding must specify what counts as material before results are consulted.

Mean-based block inference needs a plausible stationary or suitably specified locally stable process, finite relevant moments, manageable temporal dependence and adequate effective information relative to block length. Conventional asymptotic block reasoning requires increasing block length while its fraction of sample size decreases; it provides no universal minimum number of trades or months. A sample with structural breaks, extremely heavy tails, tiny effective sample size or a degenerate estimator may not support the intended approximation. No single stationarity test certifies these assumptions. A causal regime label is useful description but does not automatically repair the distributional problem; ex-post slicing until one regime passes is selection.

If these conditions cannot be justified, retain descriptive results, stress scenarios and uncertainty limitations; classify the inferential comparison as not evaluable/inconclusive. Do not replace the method after viewing holdout with an iid bootstrap, trade shuffle, Gaussian t-test or favorable regime. Any different estimator or robust-tail procedure needs a prospectively amended protocol and independent data where required. This gate is a concrete prerequisite to V's inference, not a reason to invent a sample now.

## Fixed family and interpretation

Where both incremental and absolute-cash inequalities are required as one claim, use the intersection-union p-value `max(p_gain, p_cash)`; rejecting requires both necessary inequalities. This avoids silently treating the cash inequality as an additional stand-alone confirmation. Its validity still requires each component test to be valid.

For the proposed first final evaluation, C1, C3-I and C3-X form one family of **three claims**, at `alpha = 0.05` family-wise error rate. This is a conventional, explicit error-budget choice, not a success probability. Order the three valid raw p-values, compare successively with `0.05/3`, `0.05/2`, `0.05`, and stop rejecting at the first failure; preserve IDs when mapping results back. Absent/nonadmitted claims retain a conservative p-value of one for adjustment and their true status remains “not evaluated.” Do not shrink the family after observing results. Adding a claim changes the family and must precede final access.

Holm allows dependence among the tests; it **cannot fix invalid marginal p-values**, look-ahead, repeated holdout use or omitted variants. Its nominal FWER therefore remains conditional on the validity/approximation of the individual inferential procedures. Development variants, horizons, hyperparameters and manual choices still belong in the trial ledger and full chronological selection procedure. A single selected-model test on repeatedly reused validation data cannot be promoted to a final claim by applying Holm to three names. C2's secondary risk comparison does not acquire an alpha claim automatically; its own objective and additional confirmatory testing would require an explicit error budget before results.

## Reused methodology and unresolved originals

The [existing methodological assessment](../../research/evidence-methodology.md) is reused with its recorded scope and source identities:

- **MET-03 / DSR:** author manuscript 2014-07-31, relevant pp.7–11 and appendix A.3 already examined. DSR needs trial dispersion/count assumptions, estimable moments and dependence/frequency treatment. It remains a conditional diagnostic; neither its value nor an effective-trial approximation is a probability of future profitability. No DSR was computed here.
- **MET-04 / PBO:** author manuscript 2015-02-27, §§2.1–2.2 and stated limitations already examined. CSCV can conflict with strictly chronological learning under dependence, requires all alternatives, and measures ranking selection rather than absolute profit. It does not replace walk-forward or sealed final evaluation. No PBO was computed here.
- **MET-05 / multiplicity:** NBER WP20592, 2014-10, designated sections already examined. FWER/FDR and unpublished-search concerns support trial registration; the paper's factor-market t-threshold is not adopted as a crypto rule.
- **MET-01 / White, MET-02 / Hansen, MET-06 / Lo:** remain metadata/partial-access references. This review does not claim newly read original formulas, nor make Reality Check/SPA or a quoted Sharpe correction mandatory. Targeted original-method access would be required before claiming to implement those specific procedures faithfully.

The three-page budget is exhausted and sufficient for these limited design distinctions. It is not a theoretical proof, an implementation certification or a reason to expand Phase I. Runtime implementation/calibration and complete sample binding remain gates for separately authorized work.
